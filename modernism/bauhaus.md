## What is it?

The Bauhaus was a German school of art, design, and architecture founded by Walter Gropius in 1919 and closed under Nazi pressure in 1933. Its changing workshops brought artists, craftspeople, and architects together to explore how form, material, function, and industrial production could relate. Bauhaus work is diverse; it is not simply a palette of primary colors and circles.

## How do you recognize or use it?

In graphic design, Bauhaus-influenced work often uses geometric forms, asymmetrical balance, sans-serif typography, photography or photomontage, and strong relationships between type and image. These devices can give a persuasive page a direct, constructed feel: a clear headline and visual diagram can explain how an offer works. Bauhaus principles also invite questions about usefulness and access, rather than decoration alone. Avoid flattening the school’s varied history into a generic “modern” look.

## Examples

### 1. Joost Schmidt, *Staatliches Bauhaus Ausstellung*, 1923

The [MoMA object record](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/6235) identifies Schmidt’s exhibition poster as a 1923 lithograph printed by von Reineck & Klein in Weimar. Broad geometric arcs and diagonals carry the lettering through the composition, so typography and image work as one visual structure. For a persuasive page, this integrated hierarchy can make a headline feel like part of the product’s visual explanation. Image: linked MoMA collection record; reproduction permissions must be checked with MoMA/Scala.

### 2. Walter Dexel, *Bauhaus Ausstellung*, 1923

The [MoMA collection record](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/7845) describes Dexel’s work as letterpress on paper, mounted on board, and names the Bauhaus in Dessau as printer. The stark black-and-white letterforms and compressed information make this a useful contrast to Schmidt’s more geometric poster: Bauhaus graphic experiments did not follow one fixed recipe. A contemporary persuasive design can borrow the compact, high-contrast hierarchy when it needs to make essential information quickly scannable.
