# Scarcity
[Back to persuasion](README.md) · [Home](../README.md)

## What is it?

Scarcity is the increased appeal an opportunity can have when access, time, quantity, or availability is genuinely limited. Cialdini describes scarcity as a cue that can make people value an option more or fear losing access to it. A scarce offer is not automatically valuable, and artificial limits can damage trust.

## How do you recognize or use it?

Common forms include a real application deadline, a limited-seat event, a finite production run, or inventory that is actually running low. State what is limited, why, and when the limit ends; keep the information current. Avoid resetting countdowns, false “only one left” claims, or presenting routine stock as a special deadline. Scarcity can help audiences make a timely decision, but urgency should not conceal costs or prevent a considered choice.

## Examples

**Hypothetical application:** a community ceramics studio has 12 places in a Saturday beginner workshop because it has 12 wheels. Its page could say: “12 places for the October 24 workshop; registration closes October 20 or when full.” The cap and cutoff should match the studio’s actual booking system, and registration should close or update when the places are taken. The example gives a concrete reason and boundary for the scarcity claim instead of inventing demand.
