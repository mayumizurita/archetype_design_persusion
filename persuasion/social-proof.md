# Social Proof
[Back to persuasion](README.md) · [Home](../README.md)

## What is it?

Social proof is the tendency to use other people’s choices or behavior as a cue when deciding what to do, especially when a situation is uncertain. In Cialdini’s framework, evidence is most relevant when it comes from people similar to the audience or from a genuinely informative group. Popularity alone does not prove quality, safety, or fit.

## How do you recognize or use it?

Common forms include verified customer reviews, accurate usage counts, peer recommendations, adoption by a relevant organization, and examples of people using a service. Make the source, date, and context clear. Do not fabricate reviews, inflate numbers, hide negative context, or imply that the majority has tested something when it has not. Social proof can reduce uncertainty, but it can also pressure people to conform.

## Examples

**Hypothetical application:** a neighborhood repair workshop has completed 240 documented repairs this year. Its page might say: “Join neighbors who have already repaired 240 items with us this year.” The number should be linked to a current, auditable workshop record, and the page should clarify whether the count means items repaired, appointments, or attendees. This provides a relevant behavioral cue while avoiding a fictional testimonial. If no verified count exists, use a real participant story with permission or leave out the claim.
