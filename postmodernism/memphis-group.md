# Memphis Group
[Back to postmodernist design](../postmodernism/README.md) · [Home](../README.md)

## What is it?

Memphis was an Italian-based design group founded in Milan in 1981 around Ettore Sottsass. Its furniture and objects challenged modernist expectations of neutral, functional good taste with bright color, graphic pattern, playful geometry, unusual materials, and deliberate combinations of historical references. Memphis objects often function as statements as much as practical furnishings; the group dissolved in 1987, but its visual language continues to be adapted.

## How do you recognize or use it?

Look for clashing or vivid colors, repeated dots and stripes, asymmetry, simple geometric parts, laminate or other conspicuous surface treatments, and witty juxtapositions. In persuasive design, Memphis-like contrast can make a campaign memorable and give a brand a playful, nonconformist voice. Keep the core offer and CTA readable: visual energy may attract attention, but it can also compete with the action or make a serious promise seem unserious.

## Examples

### 1. Ettore Sottsass, *Carlton*, 1981

The [Metropolitan Museum of Art collection record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/486989) identifies Sottsass’s Carlton room divider with the Memphis Group. Its angled, brightly colored planes turn a functional divider into a highly visible sculptural object. As a persuasive design reference, it demonstrates how a recognizable shape and color system can create brand distinction; a page adapting this vocabulary still needs a calm enough area for its offer and CTA.

### 2. Ettore Sottsass, *Lampe de table Tahiti* (design drawing), 1981

The [Centre Pompidou collection record](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/MzJjset) identifies this 1981 design drawing for the lamp as a work by Sottsass and part of a set of Memphis lamp studies. The drawing turns an everyday light into an unexpected sculptural character through angled parts and vivid colored forms. That contrast can help a persuasive message remain memorable, although novelty should support the product’s real benefit rather than substitute for it.
